What the four terms mean
Reformed
Salvation is God's work from first to last. He chose his people before creation without regard to anything in them, Christ died to secure their salvation, the Spirit gives them new birth and faith, and God keeps them to the end. Stated at the Synod of Dort (1618–19) and in the Westminster Confession (1646). Also called Calvinist.
Arminian
God's grace goes before every person and makes a free response possible. Christ died for all, God elects those he foreknows will believe, and grace can be resisted. Set out in the Five Articles of the Remonstrance (1610) after Jacobus Arminius and developed by John Wesley. The Remonstrants left open whether a believer can fall away. Wesley taught that one can.
Cessationist
The miraculous sign gifts (tongues, prophecy, healing) confirmed the apostles' message and ended with the apostolic age. God still answers prayer and may heal. Argued at length by B. B. Warfield in Counterfeit Miracles (1918).
Continuationist
All the gifts of the Spirit continue until Christ returns and should be sought and tested by Scripture. Held by Pentecostal churches since the Azusa Street revival (1906), by the charismatic renewal, and by Reformed continuationists such as Wayne Grudem.
